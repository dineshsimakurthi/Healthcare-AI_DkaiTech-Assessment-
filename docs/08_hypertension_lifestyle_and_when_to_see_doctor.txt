Hypertension: Lifestyle Changes and When to See a Doctor
Based on public guidance from WHO and the American Heart Association (summarised for this demo).

Lifestyle changes that lower blood pressure:
- Reduce salt: WHO recommends less than 5 grams of salt (about 2 grams of sodium) per day.
- Follow the DASH eating pattern: plenty of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy, and limited red meat and sweets.
- Eat potassium-rich foods such as bananas, beans and leafy greens, unless a doctor advises otherwise.
- Maintain a healthy weight; losing even 1 kg can lower blood pressure slightly.
- Exercise at least 150 minutes a week.
- Limit alcohol and do not smoke.
- Manage stress and get enough sleep.

When to consult a doctor:
- Blood pressure readings of 130/80 mmHg or higher on repeated measurements.
- Blood glucose readings above the normal range, or any symptoms of diabetes such as excessive thirst, frequent urination or unexplained weight loss.
- You have risk factors (family history, obesity, age over 40) and have never been screened.
- Wounds on the feet that do not heal, or vision changes, if you have diabetes.

Seek emergency care immediately for: chest pain, severe shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, difficulty speaking, sudden severe headache, blood pressure above 180/120 with symptoms, confusion, fainting, or fruity breath with vomiting in a person with diabetes.
