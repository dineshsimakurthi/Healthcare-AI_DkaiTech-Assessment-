Symptoms of Diabetes
Based on public guidance from WHO, CDC and NIDDK (summarised for this demo).

Common symptoms of high blood glucose include:
- Frequent urination, especially at night
- Increased thirst and a dry mouth
- Increased hunger
- Unexplained weight loss (more typical of type 1 diabetes)
- Extreme tiredness and fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing cuts, sores or frequent infections
- Tingling, pain or numbness in the hands or feet
- Dark, velvety patches of skin around the neck or armpits (acanthosis nigricans), a sign of insulin resistance

Type 1 diabetes symptoms often appear suddenly over days or weeks. Type 2 diabetes symptoms develop slowly and may be mild, so many people have no symptoms at all and only find out through a blood test. This is why screening is recommended for adults who are overweight or over the age of 35.
