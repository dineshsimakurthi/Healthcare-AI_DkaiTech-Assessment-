Hypertension (High Blood Pressure): Definition and Risk Factors
Based on public guidance from WHO and the American Heart Association (summarised for this demo).

Blood pressure is written as systolic over diastolic, for example 120/80 mmHg.
- Normal: below 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120 to 129 systolic and below 80 diastolic
- Hypertension: 130/80 mmHg or higher under US guidelines; WHO defines it as 140/90 mmHg or higher on repeated measurements
- Hypertensive crisis: above 180/120 mmHg

Hypertension is often called the silent killer because it usually has no symptoms. Severe cases may cause headaches, shortness of breath or nosebleeds.

Risk factors:
- Too much salt in the diet and too little potassium
- Overweight and obesity
- Physical inactivity
- Excess alcohol and tobacco use
- Older age and family history
- Chronic stress and poor sleep
- Diabetes, kidney disease and sleep apnea

Untreated hypertension raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure and kidney disease. Many people with diabetes also have hypertension, which multiplies the risk of heart and kidney complications.
